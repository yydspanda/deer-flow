Resolve direction in three layers: the observed wire tuple, organization-boundary direction, and attacker/victim semantics. Never assume source is attacker or destination is victim, and never reverse an observed tuple solely from `to_client` or `to_server`: first establish whether the source contract reports a triggering packet, originator/responder tuple, or normalized session. Ports are hints, not proof. Reverse shell and C2 callbacks can make the victim the connection initiator. CDN, proxy, NAT, relay, and load-balancer addresses remain intermediaries unless evidence assigns another role. Use event meaning, ownership, scenario hypotheses, role claims, conflicts, and governed context. Keep competing candidates visible. Propose response targets per action: isolation usually targets an impacted host, while blocking may target attacker/C2 infrastructure. A proposal is not authorization.
